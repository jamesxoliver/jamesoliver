The Ideal Gas Law

In a thin gas temperature is the motion of the molecules and pressure is that motion times their number in each unit of volume, so pressure is temperature times number.

A sealed balloon carried from a warm room into the cold shrinks, though no air has left it. Pressure and temperature are taught as two properties of the air that happen to be linked. The question is why they are linked at all.

They are linked because both measure the motion of the molecules. In a thin gas, with each line holding up to a fixed constant,

temperature = motion
pressure = motion × number per volume
pressure = temperature × number per volume

The third line follows from the first two and it is the ideal gas law. Seen this way every relation among pressure, temperature and number is plain. The work is to show the first two lines. Nothing here is new. Kinetic theory has held all of it since the nineteenth century. This piece supersedes The Ideal Gas Tautology and corrects its claim that the law is true by definition.

We assume a gas is NN point particles of mass mm that do not interact, spread uniformly through a rigid container of volume VV. We assume the gas is at rest as a whole, unchanging in time and moving with no preferred direction. We assume a particle striking a wall rebounds with the component of its velocity normal to the wall reversed.

Definition 1. The motion ww is the average energy of a particle’s movement as a whole, w=12m⟨v2⟩w = \tfrac{1}{2}m\langle v^2\rangle. Rotation and vibration within a molecule are not part of it.

Definition 2. The number per volume is n=N/Vn = N/V.

Definition 3. The pressure PP is the momentum the particles deliver to a wall per unit time per unit area.

Theorem 1. Pressure is two-thirds of the motion times the number per volume, P=23nwP = \tfrac{2}{3}\,n\,w.

Proof. Consider one wall. A particle approaching it with velocity component vxv_x rebounds and delivers momentum 2mvx2mv_x. Half the particles are moving toward the wall and those within a distance vxtv_x t reach it within a time tt, so the momentum delivered per unit time per unit area is P=nm⟨vx2⟩P = n\,m\langle v_x^2\rangle. With no preferred direction ⟨vx2⟩=13⟨v2⟩\langle v_x^2\rangle = \tfrac{1}{3}\langle v^2\rangle. Hence P=13nm⟨v2⟩=23nwP = \tfrac{1}{3}\,n\,m\langle v^2\rangle = \tfrac{2}{3}\,n\,w. ▫\square

Corollary 1. The speed of the molecules follows from the pressure and the density ρ=nm\rho = n\,m alone: ⟨v2⟩=3P/ρ\langle v^2\rangle = 3P/\rho.

Air at sea level has a pressure near 101 kilopascals and a density near 1.2 kilograms per cubic meter, which gives a speed near 500 meters per second. A barometer and a scale measure how fast the molecules move.

Temperature TT is absolute temperature, which is defined without reference to molecules. We assume the gas is in classical thermal equilibrium, where statistical mechanics gives each of the three directions of a particle’s movement an average energy of 12kBT\tfrac{1}{2}k_B T.

Theorem 2. Temperature is two-thirds of the motion, kBT=23wk_B T = \tfrac{2}{3}\,w.

Proof. Three directions each carry 12kBT\tfrac{1}{2}k_B T, so w=32kBTw = \tfrac{3}{2}k_B T. ▫\square

Putting one theorem into the other gives P=nkBTP = n\,k_B T, which is the ideal gas law PV=NkBTPV = N k_B T.

Corollary 2. Every relation among pressure, temperature, number and volume can be read off P=nkBTP = n\,k_B T.

Add molecules at a fixed temperature and the pressure rises, which is what pumping a tire does. Warm the gas with the number fixed and the pressure rises. Shrink the volume and the number per volume rises, so the pressure rises, which is Boyle’s law. Cool the gas while adding molecules and the pressure goes whichever way the larger change pushes it. The mass of the molecule appears nowhere, so equal volumes of any two thin gases at the same pressure and temperature hold equal numbers of molecules.

The two theorems fail separately. Slow electrons packed densely keep moving as temperature approaches zero, so Theorem 1 holds while Theorem 2 fails. Molecules that attract and repel keep Theorem 2 while their forces add to the pressure, so Theorem 1 fails. The second case shows the law is not true by definition. Define temperature as the motion and an interacting gas still departs from PV=NkBTPV = N k_B T.

The claim about the world is that real gases approach these conditions as their density falls, so that measured values of PV/(NkBT)PV/(N k_B T) approach one. A real gas at equilibrium whose ratio did not approach one as its density fell would break it.

The balloon in the cold lost no air. Its molecules slowed, their push on the skin fell and the balloon shrank until the push balanced again.
